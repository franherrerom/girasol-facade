Problem

Girasol is a skin of triangular modules on a south-facing facade in Miami. Each module has three leaves that open toward the sun.

My website shows one fixed version; I never tested other module sizes or openings. What I most want to find out is whether the skin really gives energy. The answer changes with the season and the hour: an opening that sees out well in March can cost energy in December.

It's for the people working on the floors behind the skin, who need to see out; the building owner, who wants the skin to make real energy; and the crews who maintain about 3,000 motors.

What must stay true — shown as warnings

  1. At eye level, the hole you see through a module is never under 6 in.
  2. At eye level, at least 25% of the facade is clear.
  3. Over a clear day, the skin catches at least as much sun as the same surface closed.
  4. Leaf tips never reach more than 1.5 ft past the frame.

Findings

Girasol started as a picture: a building that opens toward the sun like a sunflower. My website said each module made 180 W and the skin caught 2.6 times more sun than a closed one. Those numbers were part of the concept. I never measured them.

So I built a tool that measures. It places the real sun over Miami for any hour in June, March or December, works out how squarely it hits every leaf on every face, and compares that with the same surface closed. The answer was humbling. A module gives about 40 W at best, not 180. Opening where the sun hits squarely looks like a sunflower, but it tilts the leaves away from the sun, so in March and December it catches a little less than staying shut.

That changed what the skin is for. Opening is for light and view, not for more energy. The version I chose lets the whole skin follow the sun with one rule, on any surface, and I check the view rules at eye level to make sure people inside can still see out. The tool also showed who the design leaves out: someone looking out at an angle sees a smaller hole than my 6 in rule measures, and it doesn't check that yet.
